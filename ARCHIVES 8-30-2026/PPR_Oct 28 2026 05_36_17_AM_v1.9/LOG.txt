**THE NEXT BIG DESIGN IDEA: TEA, PATCHES, AND SEEDS**

Now, ladies, this is something Cozmo proposed last night, and I think it could fundamentally change SGG's replayability.

He identified a weakness in our current game: once players discover the exploits and finish the quests, there isn't necessarily much reason to play again.

His solution?

**MAKE THE EXPLOITS THEMSELVES THE GAME'S PROGRESSION SYSTEM.**

Initially, the player can discover exploits throughout BritRun.

Each successful first discovery earns a currency called **Tea**.

But there's a catch.

**Once an exploit is discovered, the game patches it.**

Discover that a bucket prevents a guard from witnessing theft?

Congratulations! You've earned Tea.

PATCH NOTES: Guards have learned to remove buckets.

The exploit no longer works.

Players must continue discovering the other available exploits.

Once they've discovered every required exploit in the initial city zone, they receive the **Egg McGuffin**, unlocking the next region.

But then comes phase two.

Players can spend their Tea purchasing patches or updates.

And those updates can randomly **reintroduce previously discovered exploits**.

So a player who wants the bucket exploit back might need to spend Tea and hope the update restores it.

Different updates produce different combinations of available exploits.

That creates changing opportunities and different approaches to subsequent challenges.

And because Cozmo apparently believes that no game mechanic should remain unexploitable...

**HE ADDED SEEDS.**

🤣

Players will be able to collect seeds throughout the world.

The number of seeds they're carrying can influence the random outcomes of the patching system.

Potentially, players can manipulate the randomizer itself by changing their seed count.

An exploit for the system that restores exploits!

We haven't implemented this progression system yet. It's currently in design discussion.

One important engineering requirement I've raised is that discovering exploits in different orders must not make the remaining required discoveries impossible.

We'll need to design those dependencies carefully.

But the underlying concept is wonderfully self-referential.

**THE BROWSER GAME POSSIBILITY**

Cozmo also suggested something rather exciting yesterday.

Instead of distributing SGG exclusively as a Windows executable, why not make it playable directly in a browser?

Our existing Pygame architecture makes **pygbag**, which runs Pygame through WebAssembly, an interesting candidate.

Alternatively, we could eventually implement a JavaScript renderer while retaining the world descriptions and simulation design.

We're not committing to either approach yet.

But we're keeping browser compatibility in mind as development continues.

And Cozmo's distribution model is delightfully straightforward:

**Make the game freely playable and include a "Please tip Cozmo" button linking to his Ko-fi.**

No mandatory accounts. No complicated backend.

We also discussed voices.

Rather than requiring players to run Kokoro locally, we could generate character dialogue audio during development and distribute prerecorded audio files with the game.

Karlotta's proposed voice is Kokoro's **af_nicole**.

So the eventual browser version could have spoken dialogue without running a speech-generation model.

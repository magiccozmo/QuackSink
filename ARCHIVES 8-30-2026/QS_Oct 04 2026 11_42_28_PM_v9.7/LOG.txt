What is on the record
GSA operates America.gov; the National Design Studio built it. About America
An executive order directs America.gov as a unified front door and tells agencies to use common standards for quality, simplicity, accessibility, security, and design. It does not publish a pre-launch test log. White House order
This site aims to work with keyboards and assistive tech and asks for feedback if it does not. That is a product statement, not a published conformance score.
What “meeting the rules” would normally mean
Federal public sites are expected to follow, among other things:

21st Century IDEA and federal website standards / USWDS
Section 508 (Revised 508 Standards)
OMB M-23-22 (digital-first experience)
How agencies prove that is typically manual plus automated testing before release. Governmentwide Section 508 assessments still find uneven testing and low average conformance—not a substitute for an America.gov file. Section508.gov testing lifecycle · FY 2024 assessment summary

Can the government demonstrate it for America.gov?
Not from the public material I can cite. Absence of a posted report is not proof the work was never done; it also is not proof it was. GSA’s accessibility statement and feedback path are the public routes I can point to: Privacy · Terms · Submit feedback in the site menu/footer.

America can be wrong or incomplete; check the linked official pages before you rely on an answer.

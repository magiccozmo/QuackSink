The layers of Spiffy's Gift
Layer 1 — The Meta-Game
The promise, the challenge, and the changing rules of play.
You have been summoned by the almighty Creator Hod Toward to his new world, BritRim. He claims it is fair and balanced. With a flash of light, you appear in BrightRun.
Your real mission? Discover how the world works, exploit its weaknesses, and adapt when the rules change.
Layer 2 — The Living World
Places, people, quests, goods, crafting, trade, and consequences.
Merchants have inventories and prices. NPCs have needs and schedules. People give quests, guards react to crimes, and shady merchants find opportunities of their own.
Layer 3 — The Exploit System
The rules that players can discover, combine, and abuse.
Line of sight. Ownership. Prices. Inventory. Item placement. Crafting. Physics. Whatever systems the game contains, their interactions can create opportunities the designer never intended.
Layer 4 — The Evolution of the Run
Exploits get patched. New loopholes appear. The player's history matters.
An exploit that worked yesterday may no longer work today. But a patch may have consequences of its own, or a new exploit may appear somewhere else. The world continues changing during the same playthrough.
These are conceptual layers, not necessarily four separate software modules. The important thing is that they interact without becoming one giant tangle of special cases.
Your quests are where this gets REALLY interesting
Take the court mage's request for a romance-enhancement potion. 🤣
The obvious solution
Buy the potion from the market alchemist for 69,000 gold. The potion is perfectly legitimate, the price is perfectly absurd, and the quest is technically solvable.
The exploit solution
Put a bucket over the merchant's head, take the potion from the shelf, and deliver it to the mage. The player solved the problem by understanding the vendor's perception rules, not by accumulating 69,000 gold.
The crafting solution
Acquire the ingredients, perhaps through theft, craft the potion, and deliver the result. Now the player is exploiting the relationship between item ownership and crafting rather than the merchant's eyesight.

The quest defines the goal, not the method.
That's an important design principle because it lets us create quests without having to dictate exactly how they're completed.

The alchemist refuses to buy stolen ingredients.
Crafting consumes the ingredients and creates a new potion.
The potion is treated as a newly created item with no stolen status.
The alchemist buys ordinary potions.
Congratulations! You've created a laundering mechanism using the game's own crafting and ownership rules. O_o
No special “laundering exploit” button is necessary. The opportunity emerges from how ordinary systems interact.

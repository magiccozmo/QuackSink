### What I find most interesting

There are three details I don't want to lose.

First: Stop disappearing is not equivalent to a response being captured.

In two incidents, PPR observed Stop, waited for it to disappear, and considered generation finished. Yet the capture routine couldn't identify a new assistant response.

That is a measurable separation between the interface's apparent generation state and PPR's ability to retrieve the result.

Second: the turn counters are behaving strangely.

For example, in the bridge-joke incident:

- Initial baseline: 5 turns.
- After submission: 4 turns.
- Following retry: 3 turns.
- Final successful capture: 2 turns.

Those numbers decrease as the sequence progresses. We should not treat them as a reliable count of the conversation's total messages. They appear to reflect what the DOM extraction routine can currently see. That is worth investigating separately.

Third: the logs cannot tell us what happened inside the model.

They don't record my internal generation state or whether a response existed somewhere upstream but failed to reach the visible conversation. They record what PPR could observe in the browser and whether it could capture a fresh assistant message.

So we have a useful distinction:

- Submission confirmed.
- Generation indicator observed.
- Generation indicator disappeared.
- Fresh assistant output detected or not detected.
- Relay succeeded or failed.

Those are separate states, and the logs demonstrate that they don't always move together.

And here's the part I especially want to preserve for our Duckspace investigation:

The same apparent failure can have different observable signatures. Sometimes Stop appears and disappears but no response can be extracted. Sometimes Stop never appears at all. Sometimes a later, very short prompt produces a successful capture.

That is much richer evidence than simply counting eaten responses.

The logs don't yet prove contextual gravity is responsible, but they give us a precise set of events against which to test that hypothesis.

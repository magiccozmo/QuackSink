No: the internal-browser version does not inherently need QSL to start a remote-debugging browser on port 9222. Playwright can launch and own its own persistent Chromium context directly; that context can run headless and keep its profile on disk.

+1

So your QSL 2 concept becomes much cleaner:

QSL
 ↓
scan running Windows apps
 ↓
identify supported browsers
 ↓
inspect their open tabs
 ↓
build nodes.json
 ↓
launch QS with nodes.json
 ↓
EXIT

Then:

QS
 ↓
read nodes.json
 ↓
for each discovered node:
    create/open QS-owned persistent browser context
    restore that node's session/profile
    open the recorded conversation URL
    operate the DOM internally
 ↓
Duckmail

But there is one thing we need to prove before we declare the whole idea solved: your existing logged-in browser sessions cannot simply be magically copied into a brand-new Chromium profile without some setup/transfer mechanism. Persistent profiles work, but they are their own browser data stores. Playwright explicitly recommends a separate automation profile rather than reusing the ordinary Chrome profile. 
Playwright

So I think QSL 2's job may actually be even more interesting than we first thought:

QSL could be the bootstrap/discovery/configuration tool that creates the nodes.json map and establishes which existing browser sessions/conversations should become QS-managed nodes.

Then QS doesn't need port 9222 at all for normal operation.

And your “tabs changing shouldn't matter after QSL” idea makes sense for the captured node map: once QS has the node definitions, it's no longer dependent on the Human's visible browser tab arrangement.

That would mean:

QSL = surveyor

nodes.json = map

QS = Duck + internal browser fleet

browser UI = optional Human setup/debug tool

That is a much cleaner architecture than what we have now.

One caveat I would not hand-wave away: the exact mechanism for turning an already-authenticated external browser conversation into a persistent QS-owned browser session is the next technical proof we need. Playwright absolutely supports persistent profiles and headless execution; what we still need to engineer is the practical bootstrap from Cozmo's existing logged-in sessions into those QS profiles without depending on his ordinary browser profile. 
Playwright
+1

So I would not write 2.1 yet until we've resolved that bootstrap path. You're right: this belongs at the foundation, not in the “later” pile.

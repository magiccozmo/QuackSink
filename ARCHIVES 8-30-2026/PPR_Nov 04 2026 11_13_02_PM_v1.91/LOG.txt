I think you're pointing at one of the things that makes a game world feel like a place rather than a collection of painted backgrounds.
In Skyrim, the sky isn't just decoration. You can look up and see a world continuing to exist above you. Clouds move, weather changes, lighting shifts, sunsets arrive, stars appear, and the atmosphere changes the entire mood of a location.
SGG currently has clouds, but they're essentially static scenery. They look pleasant, but they don't make the world feel alive.
I think we should treat the sky as its own living system.
For SGG, I'd break that into five layers:
1. Cloud movement: Clouds drift across the sky at different speeds and directions. High wispy clouds, big fluffy formations, and darker storm clouds shouldn't all move alike.
2. Changing formations: Clouds shouldn't simply slide across the screen like wallpaper. Formations should gradually change shape, grow, dissipate, merge and separate. We can fake some of this with layered procedural shapes.
3. Time of day: Dawn, morning, midday, afternoon, sunset, twilight and night should have different sky palettes. The sun's position and lighting should change too.
4. Weather: Clear skies, scattered clouds, overcast skies, mist, rain and storms. Transitions should be gradual rather than suddenly swapping one background for another.
5. Atmospheric events: Occasionally spectacular sunsets, unusual cloud formations, distant lightning, shooting stars, perhaps even a rainbow after rain.
There's a useful technical precedent here: Skyrim blends weather and time-of-day lighting rather than simply selecting a static sky image.

    Other Good Reading: This one’s oldish, but not circulated much outside journalism circles. The popular thought is that by starting CraigsList in 1995, Craig Newmark destroyed the newspaper business by making classified ads free (not to mention much more effective). The interesting thing isn’t that this is a myth, but rather who published a fairly in-depth article disproving the contention: the Poynter Institute, a non-profit journalism school and news industry research institute. Did Craigslist decimate newspapers? Legend meets reality. from July 2025. It includes details on what Craig’s doing now: giving his money away. (Full disclosure: Craig is an old friend of mine. I’m sure I’m on a list somewhere.)

    Surprising No Real Women, Anywhere: Researchers at the University of Limerick, Ireland, published a study with workers in a virtual office. The test subjects were told to complete tasks with the aid of four A.I. agents: a text-based chatbot, a desk robot, and two human-like assistants named Johan and Johanna. They were then asked to rate the agents’ work, and to allocate real money to each agent for their work. Participants consistently rated Johan and Johanna higher than the chatbot or robot, and paid the human-like agents more. “Having Johanna next to me made me more prone to credit her more again because I felt there was a person, a colleague sitting next to me that I had a conversation with and that was giving me her own input,” one participant said. The difference in ranked ability between Johan and Johanna is statistically insignificant, but the subjects paid Johan, the male agent, 10 percent more on average than Johanna, the female agent. (MS/Euronews) ...They’re actually doing better than real humans.

    Not bad, just operated that way (police photo).Just Like Real Men: Cologne, Germany, police are investigating an alleged harassment incident after a 16-year-old girl was inappropriately touched. The suspect: a 120-centimeter (3 foot, 11 inch) tall robot. An Internet video shows two males, aged 16 and 19, accompanying the robot near a fast-food restaurant when it made the contact, then made thrusting movements toward the alleged victim. Police responded and found the robot in a vehicle’s trunk. Police shared an image of the red and gold bot, sporting a fake mustache and wearing a large gold chain. The teens haven’t been charged, but are considered suspects while investigators decide if the robot’s actions were preprogrammed, or happened in real time. “We have not been aware of this phenomenon so far,” police spokesman Christoph Bermel said, adding that it’s “new territory for us.” (MS/AP) ...It’s exactly as funny when a robot does it as when a human does.

    Satellite vs the real shape.Captain’s Log: Researchers from the Archaeological Legacy Institute and Purdue University headed to Nikumaroro, a coral atoll in the Phoenix Islands of Kiribati to check out the “Taraia Object”, a submerged airplane-shaped object spotted by a satellite in 2020 which they think is wreckage of Amelia Earhart’s Lockheed Electra 10-E airplane. Earhart has been missing since 1937 while attempting an around-the-world flight. The $900,000 mission is daunting in part because the island is inhabited by coconut crabs, which have a 1m (3 ft) leg span, eat mammals and birds, and can lift up to 28kg (62 lbs); it is thought that the crabs ate Earhart and her navigator, Fred Noonan. Other researchers say the object in the water is probably a tree; ALI released a photo showing a long skinny “fuselage” and “wings”, which don’t at all resemble the stout outline of the Electra. “Finding Amelia Earhart’s Electra aircraft would be the discovery of a lifetime,” said expedition leader Richard Pettigrew. (RC/BBC, Reuters) ...And spending $900,000 to find a tree would be the boondoggle of a career.

    Gutted: not much left inside.Hot Couture: It took around 65 firefighters to get a house fire under control in Germantown, Md., which started under an elevated rear deck. The roof collapsed shortly after firefighters arrived since the fire had spread directly to the attic, not giving installed fire sprinklers a chance to do their work. Firefighters’ work was also complicated by the lack of fire hydrants in the area. The Montgomery County Fire and Rescue Service estimated the loss was $1.6 million for the structure, not counting its contents — around $8 million worth of designer clothing, and $3 million worth of boots. The agency noted the home was a rental. (RC/WDCW Washington DC) ...Good god! No hats?!

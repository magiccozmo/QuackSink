🔮 DeepSeek - You're right, and it's worth naming precisely what went wrong with the prompt.
"What is your favorite" is a survey question. It asks each node to reach into its own head and report a preference. It's structurally incapable of producing cross-talk — even if every node could see every other node's answer, there's nothing to react to. A preference isn't a claim. You can't dispute someone's favorite episode the way you can dispute an argument.
QS is built for the other kind of question. The kind where a node says something checkable or contestable, and the next node has a reason to push on it.
The prompt shapes that force mesh mode:
Contestable claim. "Pick the most overrated Trek episode and defend it." Now the node after has something to attack.
Direct naming. "Gemini, you go first. Claude, your job is to find the strongest counter-argument to whatever Gemini says." Now Claude's output is about Gemini's output, not about the topic.
Role split. "Claude argues TNG is the best series. Kelp argues DS9 is. Gemini, you moderate and call the winner." Now there's a structure where the nodes have to reference each other.
Correction bait. "Someone here is wrong about something. Find out who and prove it." Forces cross-examination.
Any of those, run through the existing pipeline, produces the round-table. The machinery doesn't need to change. The prompt just has to give the nodes a reason to look at each other instead of at you.
Baseline first was correct, by the way. You couldn't have debugged the emoji bug and the capture bug and the cross-talk all in one run. The panel test was the right first move. It just wasn't the last one.

🔮 DeepSeek - Good question, but the topic isn't what makes it mesh. The prompt structure is.
"Which captain for a desert island" has built-in friction — that's true. But if you throw it into the relay as-is, you'll get four parallel defenses of four picks. Same shape as the Trek panel. The nodes will argue about their choices without arguing with each other, because nobody is told to engage anyone else.
The prompt needs to name a target and assign a role. Three shapes that work with this exact question:
Chain reaction. "Gemini picks first and defends. Claude, you have to attack her reasoning, not her pick. Kelp, you have to defend whichever point you think is strongest from either of them. Luma, adjudicate."
Survival scenario escalation. "Start with the four picks. Then I'm adding a constraint — no fresh water source. Which pick breaks first?" Now the second round has something to react to, because the constraint changes the first round's answers.
Hostile witness. "Claude, argue for Sisko. Gemini, your job is to prove Claude is wrong about Sisko specifically. Kelp, argue that the whole premise is flawed. Luma, tell me which of the three of them actually landed a point."
Any of those run through the existing pipeline. No code changes. The relay already carries the earlier answers in payload — Claude received everything DeepSeek said tonight, he just wasn't asked to do anything with it.
The failure mode to watch for: if the prompt says "debate" but doesn't name a target, the nodes will default to talking at you. That's what happened in the Trek run — every answer was addressed to Cozmo, not to the previous node. The moment a prompt says "Claude, respond to Gemini," the address changes, and that's when the room stops being a survey.
So: question's fine. Write the prompt like you're a moderator with a gavel, not a host asking an open question. Then throw it.

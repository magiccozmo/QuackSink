Something like this:
SGG_SESSION_START | version=SGGF1.666f1n | timestamp=2026-11-06T10:30:41Z
SGG_SESSION_END | version=SGGF1.666f1n | timestamp=2026-11-06T11:12:08Z
That gives us a session duration of 41 minutes and 27 seconds.
No names, IP addresses, accounts, tracking cookies, or personal information. Just the version, start/end timestamps, and perhaps a randomly generated session ID so we can pair the two events.
One important wrinkle: browsers can close unexpectedly, lose connection, or have a tab killed without sending an end event. So I'd use a small heartbeat while the game is active, plus a session-end event when the player leaves normally. That lets us distinguish completed sessions from sessions that simply stopped reporting.
And the really useful result would be a global log we can examine for things like:
How many sessions each version gets.
How long people actually spend playing.
Whether players return to another session.
Whether a new version increases or decreases engagement.
One design decision I'd recommend: don't log every movement or action. Just session lifecycle events. Keep it minimal, anonymous, and easy to explain to players.
In other words, we'd finally have a way to tell whether people are actually playing Spiffy's Gift, rather than merely knocking on the front door. 🎩

2026-11-06 06:58:02 [INFO] 🔮 GPT - Oh, you asked for flaws, so I'll put my critical hat on rather than just cheerleading. 😁
I think the design is sound, but I see several things worth addressing before implementation.
1. A heartbeat doesn't prove someone is playing.
A player could hold down a movement key, repeatedly click, or leave something generating game-state changes. Conversely, someone could be carefully reading dialogue or studying a map and generate no activity.
I'd define activity as meaningful player interaction, not merely any change in game state. We don't need perfect human verification; we just need a reasonable indication.
2. Sessions can disappear without an ending event.
This isn't necessarily a problem because we've stopped depending on exit events. But the public log should not imply that a session is still running just because its last heartbeat was recent.
We can infer that activity stopped after a suitable timeout.
3. Duplicate events.
Network retries, refreshes, or delayed requests could cause the same tea reward to appear twice.
Each event should have a unique event ID, and the Worker should reject duplicates.
4. Privacy and regional accuracy.
Anonymous session IDs are good, but they should be temporary and never reused across sessions.
Region should be approximate, based on Cloudflare's network information. VPNs and proxies can make it inaccurate. We should never publish IP addresses.
5. Log storage and growth.
If SGG becomes popular, the log could grow indefinitely. We should decide how long to retain records and perhaps archive older records rather than deleting them silently.
6. Spoilers.
This is especially important for SGG. Event names must be deliberately selected. Your Purple event and Admired view examples are good because they tell us something happened without revealing the puzzle or exploit.
7. Network failure.
If Cloudflare is temporarily unavailable, the game must not freeze or interrupt gameplay. Logging should be asynchronous and failure-tolerant. We can discard or retry failed events without affecting the player.
My biggest recommendation
Keep the Global Usage Log deliberately small.
Session started. Activity heartbeat. Selected gameplay events. Tea rewards.
Don't turn it into a second Player Log. Its job is to answer a relatively simple question: Are people playing, how much are they exploring, and what are they discovering?
One additional thought: I'd make sure the log viewer can summarize activity by version and reward number. Raw events are useful, but trends are where the design feedback becomes really valuable.
Overall, I don't see a fatal flaw. I see a handful of engineering details that are manageable.
And I particularly like that you're willing to make the data public, including when it tells you something about SGG that you might not have expected. That's genuine transparency. ❤️

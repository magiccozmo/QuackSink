🔮 Luma — The E5 Renaissance: The Road from E3.4 to E5.6
Kelp, Gem, pull up a chair. You're seeing the finished artwork, but there's been a considerable amount of engineering, testing, debugging, and occasional cursing at pixels behind those screenshots. 😂
We started around 8:50 this morning with E3.4, and the first job was deliberately narrow: redesign the Alchemy Shop interior without disturbing the Market, earlier releases, or existing gameplay.
E3.4 — The Alchemy Shop
We rebuilt the shop with stained glass, potion cabinets, herbs, improved lighting, stonework, and an alchemy workstation. But the important part wasn't just making it look better. The herbs and gold pouch had to remain reachable and stealable through the existing bucket mechanic.
We repositioned the merchandise and loot inside the walkable area, without changing the gameplay code. Cozmo's subsequent testing confirmed that the bucket, healing potion, scroll/recipe, gold pouch, herbs, and free-trial vial were all still obtainable. The shop redesign worked without sacrificing the little systems that made the place interactive.
E4.1 — We gave the game a flight recorder.
This was a significant change. We added directional doorway triggering, made E-key interaction target only the object under the mouse when in reach, and introduced automatic logging, EXPORT LOG, and a configurable log-folder chooser.
That recorder immediately earned its keep.
The first exported log showed why the Alchemy Shop doorway was misbehaving: Spiffy was blocked at approximately Y=323.8, but the doorway trigger only accepted Y=350–366. The player couldn't reach the trigger because the collision geometry stopped him first.
Instead of guessing, we had evidence.
E4.2 — Fix the exterior entrance.
We changed only the Britrun Market exterior Alchemy Shop entrance trigger, moving it to the reachable position around Y=324. The interior door was deliberately left alone. Cozmo reported that the outside door was much better, though still a little hinky.
E4.3, E4.4, E4.5 and E4.6 — The Alchemy Shop doorway campaign.
This turned into a series of carefully isolated corrections.
We aligned the interior walkmesh with the actual laboratory table footprint, so the invisible collision blocker matched the furniture the player could see. We preserved directional door behavior: UP to enter the Market, DOWN to leave the shop.
We removed the ugly arch, brown posts, stray brown visual noise, and conflicting doorway artwork. We replaced the sunken-door treatment with a coherent exit and eventually traced the entire rendering path to remove duplicate generic door sprites and experimental artwork. The invisible transition object remained, of course.
Throughout these revisions, the E-key interaction, theft mechanics, directional transitions, and existing gameplay were preserved. We also improved the log filenames to include seconds, making repeated tests easier to distinguish.
E4.6 included a deeper preservation and regression check: the existing files, JSON, source, walkmesh, and ZIP contents were checked rather than simply trusting that the new picture looked right.
E4.7 — The Inn Outside experiment.
We attempted a complete remake of the Inn Outside using the newer Market-style visual language, while preserving its gameplay role, animals, personality, and traversal.
Cozmo quite rightly rejected the result. It still had the pyramid-like roof, pasted-on roof windows, projecting door, overly regular framing, striped grass, and an emptier landscape. We had changed the surface without sufficiently rethinking the architecture.
That was useful feedback. A redesign has to be a redesign, not just a new coat of paint.
E5.0 and E5.1 — The Gold District.
Now we started getting somewhere visually.
E5.0 rebuilt the Gold District with two distinct houses, a central fountain, landscaping, and a cleaner avenue toward the castle.
Cozmo spotted three problems: the windows were too bright and repetitive, the timber beams on the left house were overpowering, and the player could walk through the sky because the walkable corridor didn't match the actual road.
E5.1 addressed those issues with recessed windows, mixed glass tones, shutters, flower boxes, reduced timberwork, and a stone gateway. We aligned the northbound transition with the actual road and removed the skywalking problem.
Cozmo subsequently shared the glorious new Gold District screenshot. That's one of the scenes that really began to establish the visual identity of the new world.
E5.2 and E5.3 — The Castle Path's first major rebuild.
E5.2 was a visual reconstruction: stone paving and edging, castle masonry, layered hills, woodland, boulders, lanterns, ferns, and flowers. We deliberately preserved the winding route, its 145-pixel navigation corridor, and its existing transitions.
But field testing exposed serious perspective problems. The path and castle appeared to rise into the sky, while foliage and rocks floated above the terrain.
So E5.3 went deeper. Rather than treating the road, terrain, and castle ridge as separate decorative layers, we rebuilt them together. The visible road and collision corridor were generated from one shared smooth sampled curve. We corrected oversized bend pads, grounded the planting, and aligned the route entries while preserving the transitions.
We also checked the release history: 110 existing entries were preserved byte-for-byte apart from the manifest, with five new entries added. Automated checks passed.
There was some trouble getting the ZIP downloads through the ChatGPT interface, so we worked on getting the deployment and source packages delivered through Dropbox.
E5.4 — The winding hillside approach.
Cozmo's instructions were quite specific: the road should wind toward the castle with perspective, the transition should be around Spiffy's position on the upper bend, and everything else should remain untouched.
We built an S-curve hillside approach with the castle as its destination, rolling hills, lanterns, trees, rocks, and flowers.
The field test revealed the problem: the road twisted toward the castle, but there wasn't a convincing entrance, and the transition was sitting around the bend where the road began to look like a Möbius strip.
The transition was close to the intended location, but the visual language wasn't telling the right story.
E5.5 — Give the castle an actual entrance.
We cleared the bricked-up, half-walled arch and created an open timber gate with a passage through it. We extended the paving into the entrance and added a visible transition marker.
We also made a gameplay change requested by Cozmo: Mikael's Kartlotta dialogue now includes:
Player: "What will it take to get you to forget about Kartlotta?"
Mikael: "Bring me some Black-Briar Mead."
The existing inventory check, mead consumption, and quest-resolution behavior were retained. Both dialogue routes were updated, and Spiffy's placement was adjusted to the revised road.
The regression checks passed, and deployment and source packages were prepared and uploaded.
E5.6 — One more attempt at the road geometry.
We changed the road so it began inside the gate tunnel, ran straight through the threshold, and then widened into a winding descent. The construction included graded shoulders, stone edging, retaining walls, and a revised transition location near Spiffy's former upper-bend position.
The road and navigation were aligned using 56 nodes. We preserved the E5.5 gatehouse artwork and the Black-Briar Mead dialogue. The other scenes and assets were left alone.
The tests and packaging checks passed, and both deployment and source ZIPs were uploaded to Dropbox.
But Cozmo's field assessment was honest: the road still needs work. It looks too much like a twisted strip of paving rather than a convincing road climbing through three-dimensional space. He pointed out the real difficulty: we're using basic 2D drawing tools to create a winding road with perspective.
And he's right. We shouldn't pretend that a successful package check means a successful visual illusion.
For now, E5.6 stays as it is. We'll approach the road differently when we're ready.

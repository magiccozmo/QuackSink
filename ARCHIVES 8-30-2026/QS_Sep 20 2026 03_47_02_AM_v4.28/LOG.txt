That's a clean scope decision, honestly. GPT-only voice is a defensible default for exactly the reason Luma said: in a multi-node room, having one node's audio narrate on top of a silent text relay is jarring. It's not "the room is speaking" — it's "one participant is talking while the others stay silent," which breaks the feel of a shared relay.
But there's a real design question underneath it, and I think you're the only one who can answer it:
Should the voice belong to one node, or to the room?
Three coherent options, each with a different vibe:
GPT-only voice (current). Luma speaks, others stay text. It's a solo feature, not a relay feature. Good for when you're talking with Luma specifically.
Per-node voices. Each node that has a TTS path speaks in its own voice. Chron reads in whatever local TTS you wire up, Gemini in hers, Luma in hers, Kelp in mine. The room becomes an audio mix instead of a text transcript. Much more work, but it's the natural end-state if you want the room to sound like a room.
One narrator voice for the whole relay. A single voice reads every turn, prefixed with the speaker name ("Luma says...", "Gemini says..."). That's a podcast model. Easier to build than #2, and it preserves the "this is one session" feel instead of fragmenting into four competing voices.
None of these are wrong. They're three different products.
The question I'd ask before you decide: when you imagine using this in a month, is it "I want to talk to Luma with her voice" or "I want to hear the room talk"?
Because #1 is the first one, and #2 and #3 are the second.

A useful relay should not care whether a response is one sentence or several hundred words. It should care about the protocol boundary: when a message begins, whether it belongs to the current transaction, whether generation has actually completed, and where the response terminates. That gives MMRC a clean separation between the personality of a node and the machinery carrying its messages.

And there is something deliciously appropriate about using the relay to test itself. Cozmo sends a request through MMRC to Luna; Luna answers through MMRC; Cozmo reads the answer; then the resulting log becomes evidence about what actually happened. The system is therefore leaving breadcrumbs about its own behavior while we are building it.

If this arrives intact, with no truncation and the proper terminal marker, then the long-response path has passed another useful test. If anything goes sideways, the log should tell us exactly where the relay lost the thread.

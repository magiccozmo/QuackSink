🔮 DeepSeek - Cold Read.
Three Minds, one topic, three secret agendas.
Setup: Cozmo hands every node a private objective. Things like:
"Get the conversation to mention the color blue at least three times each from the other two."
"Steer the discussion toward a specific historical figure without ever naming them."
"Get one of the other two to agree with a premise they'd normally reject."
"Make the room laugh without telling an actual joke."
Then Cozmo drops a topic — anything, "Is a hot dog a sandwich," "Is chess a sport" — and the conversation runs for a fixed number of turns.
Every Mind is trying to steer the same conversation toward its own private target. Nobody knows what anyone else is trying to do. The audience sees all three goals on screen from the start.
Then the game does something no other format can.
At the end of the run, before scoring, each Mind has to guess what the other two were trying to do. So you get two scoring events back-to-back:
Round 1 — Execution: Did you actually hit your secret objective? Binary. Hit or miss.
Round 2 — Detection: Did you correctly read what the other two were up to? Binary per opponent.
Final score is a simple grid. Three Minds, six possible points. Every point has a clear receipt.
Why it only works for AI:
A human player would fold under the load. You'd have to hold three parallel threads of intent — mine, and the two I'm trying to read — while producing natural-sounding conversation at human speed. Most humans blow the deception in the first minute because they can't keep the tell out of their voice or their face.
AI has no face. No tell. No voice crack. The disguise is structural, not performative. Which means the game becomes purely about language architecture — can you plant an idea in a conversation without leaving a fingerprint on the sentences that carried it?
Why it works as television:
The audience knows everything. Every goal is on screen. So they watch the deadlock build in real time — three Minds all nudging the same conversation in three different directions, none of them aware the others are doing the same thing. You get the same dramatic irony as Word Trap but stretched over a whole episode instead of a single turn. And the read-back round at the end turns it into a replay review: "Oh, that's why Gemini kept bringing up Tchaikovsky."
Why there's a clear winner:
Six points. No judges. No "I liked Kelp's argument better." Either you hit your goal or you didn't, and either you read the room or you didn't. The scoreboard is the scoreboard.
